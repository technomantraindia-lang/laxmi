Project-specific capacity requires an engineering heat-and-steam balance.

01 · Understand

Daily output is an input.
Peak steam demand is the decision.

Boiler TPH describes a steam-generation rate under stated rating conditions. AAC production creates a changing demand: pressure build-up needs high flow, holding has a different demand, and other cycle stages need little or no fresh steam.

The correct question is not only “How large is the plant?” It is “How much fresh steam must the complete autoclaving system receive at its highest practical demand?”

Two plants with the same m³/day can require different boiler capacity because their autoclaves, cycle overlap and steam-management systems are different.

SIZING METHOD

Map the cycle.
Find the peak.

A daily total divided by operating hours gives an average rate. It can hide short periods when two autoclave operations create a much higher combined demand.

  1. 01

    Define the production basis

    Confirm required m³/day, operating hours, product mix and realistic utilisation.

  2. 02

    Define every autoclave cycle

    Record geometry, loading, pressure profile, stage timing and cycles per day.

  3. 03

    Build a time-based demand profile

    Identify the highest combined demand when pressure-build stages overlap.

  4. 04

    Credit only validated recovery

    Treat steam transfer, heat recovery and hot-condensate return separately.

  5. 05

    Check boiler-side conditions

    Confirm pressure, feedwater, fuel properties, turndown, auxiliaries and rating basis.

  6. 06

    Apply a documented margin

    Allow for credible operating variation without creating unjustified oversizing.

03 · Decide

Under-sizing can restrict cycles.
Over-sizing can weaken capital efficiency.

Undersized

Lower initial rating can become a permanent process bottleneck.

Peak demand may slow pressure build-up, disturb autoclave scheduling and restrict practical production.

Correctly engineered

Capacity and operating range align with the approved cycle schedule.

The selection balances required output, part-load operation, site conditions and a realistic expansion plan.

Excessively oversized

Extra nameplate capacity does not automatically create more AAC output.

Investment may remain unused and part-load behaviour can become commercially inefficient, depending on boiler design.

These are potential consequences, not guaranteed outcomes. Actual performance depends on boiler design, controls, fuel, maintenance and the complete steam system.

RECOVERY CLARITY

Steam transfer is not condensate return.
Measure each benefit separately.

01

Steam transfer

Moves pressurised steam between compatible autoclave cycle stages. Value depends on timing, pressure control and interlocks.

02

Condensate return

Recovers hot liquid water and sensible heat where contamination, pressure and water treatment permit safe reuse.

03

Exhaust heat recovery

Transfers thermal energy to another circuit without assuming contaminated condensate can return directly.

04

Loss reduction

Insulation, drainage and steam-trap condition affect the useful steam that actually reaches the autoclaves.

One word: pressure.
Five different meanings.

Every value must identify the measurement point and whether it is gauge or absolute pressure.

Term What it means Buyer check
Boiler design pressure Pressure basis used for the boiler’s design under the applicable code. Do not treat it as the normal operating setpoint.
Boiler working pressure Pressure at which the boiler is intended to operate within approved limits. Confirm downstream demand and allowable losses.
Steam-header pressure Pressure available in the main distribution header. Check pressure drop during peak demand.
Autoclave design pressure Pressure basis used for the autoclave pressure-vessel design. Verify approved vessel documentation.
Autoclave operating pressure Pressure used during the approved curing cycle. Connect it to the process recipe, not design pressure.

Appropriate values are project-specific and must be confirmed from approved boiler, piping, autoclave and process documentation.

INDIA PROJECT REALITY

Fuel. Water. Compliance.
All change lifecycle cost.

Fuel selection

Compare availability, calorific value, moisture, ash, storage, handling, combustion equipment and emissions-control needs—not price per tonne alone.

Feedwater and treatment

Base treatment on representative water analysis and the boiler manufacturer’s approved limits. Feedwater quality influences reliability, blowdown and maintenance.

IBR and operating responsibility

Identify applicable approvals, boiler and piping documentation, inspections, relief arrangements, interlocks, emergency shutdown and trained operating responsibility.

FUTURE CAPACITY

Plan the next stage.
Do not buy uncertainty.

Expansion-ready design does not automatically mean installing the largest boiler today. Depending on the verified load, the project may protect space and interfaces, stage multiple boilers, improve recovery or revise autoclave scheduling.

The correct option depends on expansion probability, timing, fuel system, load profile, downtime tolerance and lifecycle cost.

INVESTOR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked.
Directly answered.

01How is AAC boiler capacity calculated?+

Capacity is determined from a time-based steam-demand assessment covering autoclave geometry, loading, cycle stages, overlapping demand, feedwater conditions, validated recovery, distribution losses and the boiler rating basis.

02How many TPH are required for a 500 m³/day AAC plant?+

There is no universal answer. A final recommendation requires the autoclave configuration, loading, cycle timing, pressure profile, feedwater condition, recovery design and boiler rating basis.

03Is a 6 TPH boiler compulsory for a 500 m³/day plant?+

No fixed m³/day-to-TPH rule should be treated as final engineering. The appropriate capacity must be calculated from the project’s peak fresh-steam demand.

04What happens if the boiler is undersized?+

It may slow pressure build-up, disturb the autoclave schedule or restrict production during peak demand. The actual effect depends on the complete system and operating pattern.

05Is an oversized boiler better for expansion?+

Not automatically. Excess capacity can increase investment and may create prolonged part-load operation. Expansion provision should be based on a defined future load.

06Is steam transfer the same as condensate recovery?+

No. Steam transfer moves pressurised steam between compatible cycle stages. Condensate recovery returns hot liquid water or recovers its heat. Their constraints and benefits differ.

07What information is needed for an assessment?+

Share the production target, autoclave quantity and dimensions, cycle plan, fuel, feedwater data, existing boiler information, recovery arrangement and available drawings or operating records.
