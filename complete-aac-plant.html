Batch size establishes the production platform. Sustainable capacity depends on how the complete line performs—from casting and pre-curing to cutting, autoclaving and packing.

The direct answer

Capacity is a system outcome.
Not a single-machine claim.

Batch size is a major platform-selection factor, but it does not determine daily saleable output by itself.

The slowest integrated stage becomes the practical limit. That is why two plants described with the same batch size may deliver different sustainable production.

01 · Read the complete line

What actually decides
practical AAC capacity?

Every production stage must sustain the same flow under one consistent set of assumptions.

01

Batching & pouring

Usable batch volume and sustainable casting frequency establish the production rhythm.

02

Mould circulation

Mould quantity and pre-curing duration must sustain every planned casting cycle.

03

Cutting & transfer

Cranes, tilting and cutting must process each cake without creating a queue.

04

Autoclaving & packing

Curing, steam and finished-product handling must match the upstream flow.

Planning framework

Gross casting output = usable volume per batch × sustainable batches per hour × scheduled casting hours.

Practical saleable output must additionally reflect utilisation, downtime, product mix, process losses, breakage and quality rejection.

02 · Compare like with like

One word.
Four different volumes.

“Batch size” is often used loosely. Before comparing quotations, establish exactly which volume the supplier is quoting.

01

Mixer gross volume

The total physical mixer volume; not necessarily the safe working volume.

02

Usable batch volume

The actual slurry volume prepared and discharged in a normal cycle.

03

Net cake volume

The cake volume moving through cutting and curing after process allowances.

04

Saleable output

Accepted finished product after breakage, rejection and quality losses.

Investor check

Never compare two AAC plant proposals using batch size alone. Ask for the usable batch volume, mould dimensions, net cake volume and the assumptions used to convert batches into m³/day.

04 · Plan the expansion boundary

Start smaller.
Without designing twice.

A larger batch platform may begin at lower output when phased operation is engineered from the start. The initial design must identify what is installed now, what is sized for the future, and what can be added without major shutdown or civil modification.

Plan in Phase 1

Batch and mould platform, main material flow, expansion space, building clearances and utility routes.

Add subject to design

Autoclaves, mould inventory, boiler capacity, packing modules and selected automation functions.

More autoclaves do not always mean more production.

Adding autoclaves helps when autoclaving is the verified bottleneck and the rest of the production line has spare capacity.

Expand the constrained process—not simply the most visible machine.

05 · Define the automation scope

Semi-automatic vs fully automatic.
Compare the interlinking.

“Fully automatic” is not a sufficient capacity specification. The same label can describe very different machine and material-transfer scopes.

Semi-automatic

More operator-dependent coordination

  • Individual machines may be automatic
  • Transfers and confirmations require more operator involvement
  • Capacity depends more heavily on production coordination

Fully automatic

Interlinked production sequencing

  • PLC-controlled sequencing can reduce avoidable waiting
  • Alarms, interlocks and production data improve visibility
  • Higher output is possible only when automation removes the active constraint

Technical questions

Frequently asked
by AAC investors.

Short answers to questions that change plant configuration and quotation comparison.

Is AAC plant batch size the same as mould size? +

Not necessarily. Ask the supplier to state mixer gross volume, usable slurry volume, mould volume and net cake volume separately.

Does AAC plant capacity depend only on autoclaves? +

No. Practical output is limited by the slowest sustainable stage across material preparation, casting, pre-curing, cutting, autoclaving and packing.

Can production increase by adding more autoclaves? +

Yes—when autoclaving is the verified bottleneck and the rest of the line has spare capacity. Additional autoclaves cannot correct a restriction elsewhere.

Can a 4.32 m³ platform begin at lower output? +

It may be possible through phased engineering, provided the initial layout, main platform, utilities and expansion provisions are planned from the beginning.

Does a fully automatic plant always produce more? +

No. Automation improves throughput only when it removes the active constraint and the remaining systems can sustain the faster flow.

What capacity should be written in a machinery contract? +

Define the product, quality parameters, operating hours, utilities, raw materials, staffing, test duration, permitted downtime and acceptance method—not only a headline m³/day figure.
