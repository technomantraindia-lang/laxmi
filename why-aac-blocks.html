FOUNDATION · TECHNOLOGY

What is AAC?

Autoclaved Aerated Concrete is an engineered structural cellular material. Fine ground silica sand (or fly ash), cement, lime, water and aluminium powder are mixed.

Aluminium reacts in the alkaline mix to form millions of uniform micro-voids (0.1–1.5mm) causing the mixture to rise. The green cake is then wire-cut with millimeter precision and steam-cured under high pressure (12–14 bar at 190°C) inside an autoclave.

Autoclaving converts the slurry into tobermorite crystal structure—giving lightweight blocks permanent structural strength, low drying shrinkage, and true dimensional stability.

ENGINEERING HONESTY

Important
limitations.

01

Brittleness under edge impact: AAC has lower point-impact resistance than dense concrete. Proper pallet strapping, transport edge guards, and cautious site handling are required.

02

Capillary water absorption: Cellular suction requires thin-bed polymer adhesive (2–3mm) and breathable water-repellent external plaster; thick cement mortar impairs bonding.

03

Specialized wall fixings: Heavy cantilever loads (geysers, AC outdoor units) require dedicated nylon expansion wall plugs or chemical anchors rather than common wooden plugs.

04

Non-load bearing infills: AAC blocks are designed as non-load bearing curtain walling in RCC structures; load-bearing multi-story applications require reinforced ALC panels.

STATUTORY CODE & SPECIFICATION

Indian Standard for AAC blocks.

IS 2185 (PART 3) : 2002 CODE SUMMARY

Bureau of Indian Standards IS:2185 (Part 3) establishes mandatory specifications for Autoclaved Cellular Concrete Blocks in India—defining Grade 1 (Density 451–550 kg/m³, Compressive Strength ≥ 2.0 N/mm²) and Grade 2 (Density 551–650 kg/m³, Compressive Strength ≥ 4.0 N/mm²), drying shrinkage (≤ 0.05%), and strict dimensional variance limits.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Clear answers. No overclaims.

AAC blocks require fine ground silica sand or thermal power plant fly ash (60–70%), Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC 43/53 grade, 10–12%), quicklime (active CaO > 70%, 15–18%), gypsum (3–5%), and a small quantity of aluminium powder/paste (0.05–0.08%) as the gas-forming aerating agent.
AAC blocks are 3× lighter than red bricks (600 kg/m³ vs 1900 kg/m³), offer 3× better thermal insulation, have exact ±1mm wire-cut dimensions that save 75% joint mortar, and reduce building construction structural dead load by 30–35%.
Autoclaving cures the green block in saturated steam at 12–14 bar pressure and 190°C for 10–12 hours. This triggers a hydrothermal chemical reaction between silica and calcium hydroxide to form Tobermorite crystal—giving the block permanent structural strength and stopping post-production drying shrinkage.
AAC blocks are primarily used as non-load bearing infill walls in RCC/steel framed buildings up to high-rise levels. For low-rise load-bearing construction (up to G+2), Grade 2 AAC blocks (compressive strength ≥ 4.0 N/mm²) can be used with proper structural engineering review.
High precision wire-cutting (±1mm) allows the use of 2–3mm thin-bed polymer block jointing adhesive instead of 15mm thick cement mortar. This creates seamless thermal bridges, avoids wall cracks, and allows direct putty application without heavy sand-cement plastering.
