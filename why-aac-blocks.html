Understand the material

What is AAC?

Autoclaved Aerated Concrete is a lightweight, factory-produced masonry material. It is generally made from a silica source such as sand or fly ash, together with cement, lime, gypsum, aluminium powder and water.

Aluminium reacts in the alkaline mix and forms a cellular “green cake”. After pre-curing, the cake is wire-cut to size and cured with saturated steam inside an autoclave.

Hydrothermal curing develops hardened calcium-silicate-hydrate phases, including tobermorite. This is why AAC quality depends on the complete process—not on any single machine.

Material performance

Five practical advantages.

These advantages are opportunities—not automatic guarantees. Building design, wall thickness, workmanship and tested product properties still determine the result.

01

Lower wall dead load

Lower density can support structural optimisation when the building is recalculated for the changed loads.

02

Thermal performance

The cellular structure generally reduces heat flow compared with denser masonry.

03

Dimensional consistency

Wire cutting supports straighter walls and thinner, more consistent mortar joints.

04

Efficient installation

Large, lightweight units cover more wall area and can be cut for openings and services.

05

Fire & acoustic potential

Performance must be evaluated for the complete tested wall assembly, including joints and finishes.

Balanced assessment

Important limitations.

  • Blocks can chip or break when transportation, stacking and unloading are poorly controlled.
  • Heavy fixtures require anchors selected and designed specifically for AAC.
  • External walls require suitable finishes, joints and moisture protection.
  • Incorrect mortar, excessive chasing or weak junction detailing can contribute to cracks.
  • Load-bearing suitability depends on product grade, wall design and applicable requirements.
  • Similar-looking blocks can perform differently when density, strength, dimensions or curing vary.

Production defines performance

Why manufacturing quality matters.

Raw-material changes, inaccurate dosing, unstable reaction, poor green-cake handling, cutting errors or an inconsistent autoclave cycle can affect density, strength, dimensions and saleable yield.

Automation adds value when it reduces variation, protects the product, improves traceability or makes handling more dependable.

Frequently asked questions

Clear answers. No overclaims.

What are the main advantages of AAC blocks?

AAC offers lower wall load, useful thermal resistance, controlled dimensions and efficient installation. Results depend on product quality, design and workmanship.

What are the disadvantages of AAC blocks?

AAC requires careful handling, suitable fasteners, correct moisture detailing and trained installation. Poor manufacturing or workmanship can cause breakage, cracks or finishing issues.

Are AAC blocks better than red bricks?

AAC offers advantages in weight, thermal behaviour and dimensional consistency. Red bricks may offer local availability and familiar workmanship. Compare the complete installed wall system.

Does AAC automatically reduce structural cost?

No. Lower wall weight creates an opportunity for structural optimisation only when the structural design is recalculated around the changed loads.

Why is autoclaving important?

Autoclaving provides controlled hydrothermal curing that develops the hardened mineral structure responsible for AAC’s final properties.
