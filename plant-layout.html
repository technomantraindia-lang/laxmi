01 · CONFIGURATION COMPARISON

Straight In-Line vs. L-Shape. Matching production flow to land geometry.

The layout determines the flow of raw materials, moulds, green cakes, autoclave trolleys, and finished block dispatch across your surveyed plot.

Layout Type Ideal Plot Dimensions Material Flow Dynamic Expansion Suitability Key Engineering Advantage
Straight In-Line (Linear) Unidirectional Flow
Long & narrow plots (e.g., 60m width × 250m+ length) Unidirectional flow: Raw materials enter at North, finished blocks exit South Zero Disruption Add parallel lines seamlessly Shortest ferry cart cycle & simplest rail shunting
L-Shape Layout 90° Corner Transition
Square or wide plots (e.g., 120m width × 150m length) Preparation is perpendicular; cutting and autoclaving turn 90° into yard Axial Scaling Expands along yard axis Compact boiler placement & shorter steam headers
U-Shape (Loop) Layout Single Frontage Access
Plots with single-road frontage access Raw materials and dispatch share the same frontage with segregated gates Modular Mirror Expands in mirrored loop units Consolidates weighbridges & gate security
02 · CURING LOGISTICS

Single-door vs. double-door. Autoclave transfer rail circuits.

How cake trolleys enter and exit curing vessels dictates internal rail tracks, transfer cranes, cycle turnover, and operator safety.

CONFIG A · SINGLE-DOOR AUTOCLAVE

Single-Door End-Loading Circuit

Trolleys enter and exit from the same front door. Unloading occurs by pulling cured cake trains back out onto the front transfer rail before shunting to the de-panning bay.

Capex: Lower Mechanism Cost Suited: 150–300 m³/day
CONFIG B · DOUBLE-DOOR THROUGH-FEED

Double-Door Through-Feed Circuit

Uncured green cakes enter through the rear door; fully cured blocks exit through the front door directly into the de-panning bay, creating 100% unidirectional traffic.

Zero Cross-Traffic Flow Suited: 450–1,500+ m³/day
03 · SITE PLANNING CRITERIA

6 master zoning rules. Eliminating bottlenecks before civil construction.

Precision factory master planning eliminates cross-traffic between raw material bulkers and block transport trucks while minimizing boiler heat loss and pipe routing.

ZONE 01 · RAW MATERIALS

Raw Material Silo Yard

Locate fly ash, cement, and lime silos near the entrance gate for straightforward bulk-tanker turning radius (min 15m radius) and rapid pneumatic unloading.

Turning Radius: 15m Min Direct Bulker Ingress
ZONE 02 · BATCHING

Gravity Batching Tower

Gravity-assisted vertical batching tower (18m–22m height) allows slurry, water, and dry materials to discharge directly into the mixer with minimal conveyor wear.

Elevation: 18m–22m Gravity Slurry Feed
ZONE 03 · THERMAL BAY

Pre-Curing Bay Insulation

Pre-curing lines require insulated cladding to prevent seasonal temperature drops, maintaining 40–50°C for predictable rising kinetics and firm green cakes.

Temp: 40–50°C Regulated Insulated Enclosure
ZONE 04 · STEAM UTILITY

Boiler House & Fuel Yard

Place the steam boiler centrally relative to autoclaves to minimize steam pipe runs (<30m) and reduce thermal condensation loss, keeping fuel storage downwind.

Header Run: <30m Downwind Fuel Yard
ZONE 05 · LOGISTICS

Finished Goods Stockyard

Paved block curing yard sized for 10–15 days of production storage with wide driveways (min 8m) for forklift loading and simultaneous 10-wheel truck loading.

Storage: 10–15 Days Yield 8m Forklift Lanes
ZONE 06 · EXPANSION

Phase-2 Expansion Reserve

Earmark dedicated parallel foundation space for future autoclaves, ball mills, and curing tracks so plant capacity can double without halting existing operations.
