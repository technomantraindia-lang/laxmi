Should the lowest-priced AAC plant be rejected?
−
No. Price should be compared only after scope, assumptions, performance obligations and lifecycle responsibilities are normalized. A lower price may be commercially sound when the proposal remains technically complete and verifiable.

Is one running-plant visit sufficient?
−
One closely comparable reference provides useful evidence. Additional references improve confidence when technology, capacity, raw materials, product mix or plant age differ.

How can a buyer verify a ‘fully automatic’ claim?
−
Map the complete process and record every routine action, manual transfer, operator confirmation, fault-recovery task and quality-data entry. Compare functions rather than labels.

What is the most important capacity question?
−
Ask which assumptions convert mould volume and production cycles into saleable cubic metres per day. Without those assumptions, headline capacities are not directly comparable.

Should commissioning remain with the equipment supplier?
−
Clear accountability is more important than a particular contracting model. Every interface needs a competent, named and contractually responsible party.

Which reference plant is most useful?
−
A plant reasonably close to the proposed technology, capacity, raw-material route, product mix and automation level—and where operating records can be reviewed.
