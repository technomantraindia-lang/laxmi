01 — Understand

Automation is not the objective.
Better plant performance is.

An automation upgrade modernises selected mechanical, electrical and control operations in an existing AAC production line.

The right scope may be selective, hybrid or full-line automation. It should be chosen only after measuring equipment condition, layout constraints, operating losses, shutdown limits and the target saleable output.

Waiting

Machines or production sections regularly wait for one another.

Variation

Batching, pouring or mould-cycle timing depends too heavily on individual operators.

Handling

Manual movement creates delays, damage or reliance on a few experienced people.

Visibility

Production data, alarms, downtime causes or process records are incomplete.

These symptoms do not prove that full automation is required.

Maintenance, process correction, equipment replacement or targeted debottlenecking may need priority.

02 — Evaluate

Choose the level your plant
can operate reliably.

Higher automation can reduce repetitive manual work, but it can also increase dependence on sensors, PLC systems, instrumentation, software backups and skilled troubleshooting.

Selective automation

Best suited when

One or two measured bottlenecks limit the plant.

Main consideration

Focused investment and lower integration scope, although other constraints may remain.

Balanced approach

Hybrid automation

Best suited when

Labour-intensive or coordination-critical tasks need automation while practical manual control is retained elsewhere.

Main consideration

Balances labour reduction with technical complexity and maintainability.

Full-line automation

Best suited when

Equipment, layout, interfaces and utilities can support coordinated automatic operation.

Main consideration

Needs stronger instrumentation, maintenance discipline and trained technical supervision.

Important limitation

Automation does not automatically guarantee better quality, lower energy use or higher capacity.

AAC quality still depends on raw materials, formulation, slurry condition, mixing, pre-curing, cutting, autoclaving and laboratory control. Energy and capacity outcomes must be tied to a specific source of loss and verified under comparable operating conditions.

03 — Decide

Audit first.
Define scope second.

A sound brownfield proposal starts with evidence from the running plant. It establishes what can be retained, what must be modified, what needs replacement and where integration risk exists.

01

Establish the baseline

Record saleable output, cycle times, downtime, rejection, manpower and utility consumption.

02

Inspect the running plant

Review machinery condition, layout, controls, utilities, safety and production interfaces.

03

Identify the constraint

Separate process, equipment, handling, control and operating problems.

04

Select the upgrade level

Compare repair, selective automation, hybrid automation, full retrofit and new-line alternatives.

05

Define acceptance

Agree equipment boundaries, measurement methods, assumptions and exclusions before implementation.

06

Install and stabilise

Plan the shutdown, test safety and sequences, train teams and verify results after stabilisation.

Commercial discipline

Simple payback should use verified operating benefit.

Simple payback (months) = Total upgrade investment ÷ verified monthly operating benefit. Include only documented savings and contribution from additional saleable output after accounting for added maintenance, technical manpower, finance and recurring costs.
