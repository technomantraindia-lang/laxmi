01 · Understand

Technology is a production-system decision. Every movement matters.

Two plants with similar stated capacity can differ significantly in cake handling, cutting, automation, maintenance, product consistency and expansion potential.

01

Tilt-cake cutting

The green AAC cake is rotated approximately 90° before the main cutting sequence.

02

Non-tilting cutting

The cake is cut without the conventional 90° tilting operation. Exact handling varies by system.

03

Vertical autoclaving

The cut cake remains upright during autoclave loading and steam curing.

04

Horizontal autoclaving

The cake is transferred or tilted back to a horizontal orientation before curing.

The essential distinction

Cutting orientation and autoclaving orientation are related decisions. They are not the same decision.

TILT-CAKE

Reorient before cutting.

A controlled upright presentation can support precise wire cutting, repeatable transfer and integrated automation.

NON-TILTING

Cut in the original orientation.

The supplier must demonstrate demoulding, support, cutting access, waste removal and every remaining manual operation.

How it works

From slurry to cut cake. A controlled sequence.

A typical tilting plant coordinates cake strength, demoulding, reorientation, cutting, waste return and transfer for autoclaving.

01

Cast

Prepared AAC slurry is poured into the mould under a controlled recipe and filling sequence.

02

Pre-cure

The slurry rises and develops sufficient green strength for safe handling.

03

Demould

The mould is opened or removed according to the selected plant configuration.

04

Tilt

The handling system rotates the green cake by approximately 90° without excessive impact.

05

Cut

Controlled cutting wires divide the cake into the required block or panel dimensions.

06

Transfer

Cut waste is recovered where the design permits, and the cake moves toward autoclaving.

07

Cure and separate

After high-pressure steam curing, products are separated, handled and packed.

Technical clarification

Tilting does not make every block identical. It is one contributor to consistency—not a substitute for recipe, pre-curing, cutting and autoclaving control.

02 · Compare

One central trade-off. Two different handling logics.

The complete movement sequence matters more than the technology label. A sophisticated flat-cake system should not be treated as identical to every simplified non-tilting arrangement.

TILTING TECHNOLOGY

Controlled orientation.

Can support standardised cutting, machine interlocks and coordinated downstream movement.

  • Requires stable green cake strength
  • Tilting system and alignment need maintenance
  • May require separation after autoclaving
  • Expansion still depends on layout and utilities
NON-TILTING TECHNOLOGY

Fewer assumptions. More questions.

The exact cake support, lifting method and cutting arrangement must be verified.

  • Taller moulds in configurations evaluated by Laxmi
  • Potential cake-zone variation between blocks
  • Lifting or gripping may demand greater green strength
  • Simplified systems may retain more manual work

The comparison does not mean every tilting plant will perform well or every non-tilting system will perform poorly. Design, raw material, installation, operators and support remain decisive.

Vertical vs horizontal autoclaving

Block separation may improve with additional handling. Complexity and maintenance also increase.

Vertical cake autoclaving

A more direct handling sequence.

The cut cake remains upright during loading and curing. In some layouts this reduces reorientation stages and mechanical scope.

  • Layer sticking may require separation
  • Outcome depends on recipe and curing
  • Fewer mechanisms can simplify maintenance
Horizontal cake autoclaving

Separation benefit with added movement.

The cake is tilted back or transferred horizontally. Suitable green separation can reduce sticking in certain systems.

  • May require tilting-back equipment
  • May use cooking frames and green separation
  • Additional mechanisms require maintenance

Running-plant verification

Do not inspect only the machine. Inspect the production behaviour.

Use a running-plant visit to connect supplier claims with cake stability, cutting, separation, manpower and output control.

Is mould filling consistent and is pre-curing time controlled?

Does the cake remain stable during demoulding, tilting or lifting?

Are cutting wires correctly cleaned, tensioned and aligned?

How are bottom and side waste removed and returned?

Which cake movements are automatic, assisted or manual?

What happens when a cake is too soft or too hard?

How is the cake loaded, cured and separated after autoclaving?

Does separation create damage, breakage or excess manual work?

Are dimensions, density and compressive strength checked by batch?

Can the layout support the supplier’s promised expansion?

Investor questions

Frequently asked. Directly answered.

01What is tilting technology in an AAC plant?
Tilting technology rotates the green AAC cake by approximately 90° before the main cutting sequence. The upright cake is then cut into the required block or panel dimensions before autoclaving.
02What is non-tilting AAC plant technology?
The cake is cut without the conventional 90° tilting operation. Demoulding, support, lifting and cutting arrangements vary and must be evaluated through drawings and running-plant operation.
03Is non-tilting the same as flat-cake technology?
The terms may be used interchangeably, but should not automatically be treated as identical. Compare the complete lifting, gripping, cutting and transfer sequence.
04Does tilting make every AAC block the same quality?
No. Tilting can help distribute original cake zones through finished blocks, but consistency still depends on raw material, recipe, rising, pre-curing, cutting and autoclaving.
05What are the principal non-tilting drawbacks?
Depending on design: taller moulds, longer filling time at the same pouring rate, cake-zone variation, lifting requirements, cutting access, waste removal and more manual work in simplified systems.
06Is vertical or horizontal autoclaving better?
Neither is universally better. Vertical arrangements can simplify movement; horizontal arrangements can support separation but may require additional handling and maintenance.
07Can a semi-automatic plant be upgraded later?
Yes, when the original layout, controls, utilities, mould circulation and civil provisions were designed for future stages.
08Does a small AAC plant always require non-tilting technology?
No. Technology should not be selected from capacity alone. A small plant can use tilting where process consistency, automation or expansion justifies it.
