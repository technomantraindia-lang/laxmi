THE ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE

Not simply a crane.
A process machine.

At the tilting stage, the AAC cake has developed enough green strength for handling, but it has not achieved finished-product strength.

Sudden acceleration, harsh braking, poor alignment or uneven mould release can contribute to cracks, damaged edges, deformation or complete cake failure. The machine must therefore coordinate load handling, mould release, cake support and cutting-line transfer as one controlled sequence.

WHY CONTROL MATTERS

The difference between a successful demoulding cycle and a damaged cake can begin with one uncontrolled movement.

Machine design is only one factor. Cake hardness, mix behaviour, temperature, mould condition and cutting readiness must also be evaluated.

OPERATING SEQUENCE

From mould pickup
to cutting readiness.

The detailed sequence varies between plant designs. A final proposal should document the actual machine configuration and its interfaces with mould circulation and cutting.

01

Position the mould

The loaded mould reaches the tilting station and aligns with the designed pickup points.

02

Secure engagement

The handling device confirms engagement before the mould is lifted or rotated.

03

Control the tilt

Acceleration and deceleration are managed through the movement, not only at the end positions.

04

Release the mould

The mould body is unlocked only after the required position and sequence conditions are confirmed.

05

Support the green cake

The cake remains on, or transfers to, the support required by the configured cutting process.

06

Return the empty mould

The mould body and side plate return according to the plant's mould-circulation arrangement.

SELECT ON ENGINEERING INPUTS

The machine must fit
the plant around it.

A lifting-capacity figure alone cannot describe a tilting system. Selection begins with the mould, cake load, cutting process, building and required production rhythm.

01

Mould geometry

Dimensions, lifting points and side-plate design define the handling interface.

02

Loaded weight

The maximum loaded mould weight forms the basis for design load and component selection.

03

Required line rhythm

Target mould cycles must be defined together with travel distances and included movements.

04

Cutting arrangement

Cake orientation, support and transfer sequence must match the selected cutting philosophy.

CONTROL & FAULT RECOVERY

Safe sequence.
Clear responsibility.

The final proposal should identify the interlocks that prevent lifting, tilting or mould release in an incorrect position. It should also explain emergency stopping, controlled restart and power-failure recovery.

  • Engagement confirmation before lifting
  • Position confirmation before locking or release
  • Travel and motion limits
  • Overload and drive-protection logic
  • Prevention of conflicting movements
  • Alarm and fault indication

Exact devices, logic and applicable design standards must be confirmed for the offered configuration.

THE COMMERCIAL CONSEQUENCE

Downtime changes
the cake—not only the schedule.

A waiting cake continues to harden. A prolonged interruption may change cutting behaviour or make the cake unsuitable for normal wire cutting.

The result depends on recipe, cake condition, temperature, delay duration and cutting capability. Fault recovery, maintenance ownership and critical spare planning are therefore part of production-risk management.

EVALUATE THE COMPLETE SCOPE

Compare systems.
Not equipment names.

Two quotations can use the same product name while carrying very different engineering, control, installation and acceptance responsibilities.

Incomplete evaluation

  • Compare only lifting capacity
  • Accept an undefined cycle time
  • Treat controls as a separate detail
  • Ignore existing-plant interfaces
  • Review only a product photograph

Engineering-led evaluation

  • Confirm loaded mould and geometry
  • Define every movement in the cycle
  • Review interlocks and recovery logic
  • Document supply and client boundaries
  • Agree FAT and site-acceptance criteria

BUYER QUESTIONS

Questions worth
answering early.

What is the difference between an AAC tilting machine and an AAC tilting crane?+

The terms are often used interchangeably. For procurement, the important question is the offered function: lifting, tilting, mould release, cake support, transfer, control and cutting-line integration.

Why can an AAC green cake crack during demoulding?+

Possible causes include unsuitable green strength, sudden movement, misalignment, uneven mould release, mould condition, release preparation and cutting-process issues. Machine and process conditions should be diagnosed together.

Is the tilting angle always 90 degrees?+

Many systems rotate the loaded mould by approximately 90 degrees, but the final movement and cake orientation depend on the plant configuration. The offered angle and sequence should be confirmed in the technical proposal.

How should cycle time be compared?+

Cycle time is meaningful only when the start and end points, travel distances, load, locking sequence, operating mode and surrounding line arrangement are stated.

Can the machine be retrofitted to an existing AAC plant?+

Yes, subject to an engineering survey. Existing moulds, side plates, rails, building clearances, structural capacity, cutting interfaces and controls must be verified before finalisation.

What information is required for a proposal?+

Provide mould dimensions and type, maximum loaded weight, target cycles, cutting arrangement, building information, automation requirement, project location and whether the requirement is new-build or retrofit.
