AAC operating-cost guide

Reduce steam cost. Improve the complete system.

Steam cost is shaped by two linked questions: how economically steam is generated, and how efficiently it is used to cure each cubic metre of AAC. Improvement requires both sides to be evaluated together.

Actual outcomes depend on plant configuration, raw materials, operating discipline, fuel, water quality and curing requirements.

01 — Understand

A low-cost boiler does not automatically create low-cost steam. And efficient steam generation does not correct waste inside production.

One cost.
Two efficiencies.

Boiler efficiency measures how much useful steam is produced from the fuel consumed. Steam utilisation efficiency measures how much steam is required to produce each cubic metre of AAC blocks.

Optimising only one side delivers limited benefit. The commercial goal is to lower the effective steam cost per cubic metre while maintaining the curing conditions required for product quality.

01

Generate steam economically

Match boiler selection, fuel quality, combustion, feed-water treatment, heat recovery, condensate return and maintenance to actual operating demand.

02

Use each kilogram effectively

Control slurry and cake consistency, autoclave loading, curing cycles, leakage, insulation and valve sequencing across the production system.

02 — Evaluate

Find where energy is lost.
Before selecting the solution.

The same symptom—high fuel cost—can come from very different causes. A useful review separates generation losses from utilisation losses and considers how they interact.

01

Boiler loading and selection

An oversized or undersized boiler may spend too much time away from its efficient operating range. Selection should reflect actual and peak steam demand, not plant capacity alone.

02

Fuel and combustion

Variable fuel quality and an incorrect air-to-fuel ratio reduce combustion efficiency. The commercial impact appears as more fuel used for the same useful steam output.

03

Feed water and heat recovery

Scaling restricts heat transfer. Economisers and technically appropriate hot-condensate recovery can reduce the energy needed to bring feed water to steam-generation conditions.

04

Green cake and slurry control

Inconsistent density or cake quality can impair heat transfer, extend curing and cause product variation. Process stability matters alongside mechanical efficiency.

05

Autoclave loading and cycle

Partial loading increases steam consumed per cubic metre. Excessive heating, holding or pressure retention can add cost without creating corresponding product value.

06

Distribution and control

Leaks, failed traps, poor insulation and manual variation waste energy between boiler and autoclave. Control systems support consistency only when the underlying process is sound.

Do not change a curing cycle solely to chase lower steam consumption. Validate block strength, density, dimensional accuracy and repeatability under the actual mix design.

03 — Decide

Review in sequence.
Invest against evidence.

A practical improvement programme begins with measurement and operating discipline, then moves to equipment or automation where the data supports it.

STEP 01

Establish the baseline

Measure fuel consumed, useful steam generated, condensate returned and steam consumed per cubic metre over representative production—not a single favourable shift.

STEP 02

Separate the two systems

Evaluate boiler-house efficiency independently from production utilisation, while mapping the points where feed water, condensate, pressure and timing connect them.

STEP 03

Correct operating losses

Address leaks, insulation damage, steam-trap condition, boiler cleaning, combustion tuning, water treatment, loading discipline and process consistency.

STEP 04

Validate process changes

Trial curing-profile and sequencing changes in controlled steps. Record quality and throughput alongside energy use so savings are not achieved at the expense of saleable output.

STEP 05

Plan justified upgrades

Consider recovery systems, controls, piping modifications or boiler changes only after the constraint, expected benefit, operational limitation and verification method are clear.

Frequently asked questions

Practical answers.
Without shortcuts.

Does a high-efficiency boiler guarantee low AAC steam cost?

No. It can reduce the cost of generating steam, but overall cost can remain high if autoclaves are partially loaded, curing cycles are excessive, cake quality varies, or steam is lost through leakage and poor insulation.

Should steam consumption be compared only in kilograms per cubic metre?

That is a useful production indicator, but it should be read with product density, block specification, mix design, autoclave configuration, loading pattern, cycle conditions and saleable output.

Can PLC or SCADA reduce steam consumption?

Automation can improve repeatability of pressure, temperature, timing and valve sequencing. Its benefit depends on correct instrumentation, sound process logic, maintenance and consistent operating inputs.

Why does condensate recovery matter?

Hot condensate contains water and thermal energy that may be reused where technically appropriate. Recovery can reduce fresh-water, treatment and fuel requirements, but system design and water quality must be checked.

What should be checked before changing an autoclave cycle?

Confirm the existing pressure-temperature-time profile, loading, cake consistency, final strength, density and dimensional accuracy. Any revised cycle should be validated against required product quality.
