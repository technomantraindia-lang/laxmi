DIRECT ANSWER

Where a green cake becomes a defined product.

An AAC cutting system cuts a pre-cured green cake into the required block dimensions before autoclaving.

Cutting quality depends on more than the machine alone. Cake condition, alignment, wire positioning, blade setting and controlled movement must work together.

The cutting system should be evaluated as part of the complete AAC process—not as an isolated machine.

THE CUTTING JOURNEY

Four coordinated stages.
One controlled sequence.

01

Face trimming

A cutting wire removes the main excess material from the green cake ends. A finishing blade then refines the face and establishes the specified finished length.

02

Horizontal cutting

Arranged cutting wires establish one required product dimension. The controlled dimension depends on cake orientation and the approved cutting-system layout.

03

Vertical cutting

The cake is divided along the other required axis. Cutting movement, side-plate position and cake transfer must remain coordinated.

04

Waste recovery

Trimming and cutting waste is collected for controlled return to the waste-slurry preparation process, subject to plant trials and quality control.

FACE FINISHING

Blade angle is a small setting with a visible consequence.

Blade angle, condition and alignment can influence the finished cake face. An unsuitable setting can contribute to corner damage, surface marks, uneven finishing or excessive cutting resistance.

Engineering limitation

Blade adjustment cannot correct every defect. Cake hardness, pre-curing, movement, machine alignment and raw-material consistency must also be checked.

ACCURACY DRIVERS

Accuracy is a system outcome.
Not a single-machine claim.

01

Green-cake condition

A cake that is too soft may deform; an excessively hard cake may increase cutting resistance.

02

Wire position & tension

Spacing, condition and tension influence dimensional consistency and the quality of the cut surface.

03

Machine alignment

The side plate, transfer cart, guide system and cutting frames must remain correctly aligned.

04

Controlled movement

Starting, stopping and transfer should avoid unnecessary jerk while the pre-cured cake is being handled.

DIAGNOSTIC THINKING

Do not assign one cause too quickly.

These checks are starting points. The complete process should be reviewed before concluding that a defect originates in the cutting machine.

CUTTING WASTE RECOVERY

Recover material.
Control the process.

Green material from trimming and cutting can be collected and returned through a controlled waste-slurry system.

The usable proportion must not be treated as a fixed percentage. It depends on raw materials, recipe, recovered-slurry density, water balance, contamination control and quality results.

Establish the permissible recovered-slurry proportion through plant trials and quality control.

CONVENTIONAL

Cutting-trench circulation

Waste falls into a trench and is circulated toward waste-slurry preparation. Cleaning, pump condition, settlement and pipeline blockage require regular attention.

CONFIGURATION OPTION

Separate waste transfer

Where layout and process design permit, green waste can be transferred separately toward the slurry-handling area to reduce direct slurry accumulation in the cutting trench.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions serious AAC investors ask.

What is an AAC cutting system? +

An AAC cutting system cuts a pre-cured green AAC cake into the required product dimensions before autoclaving. Depending on the plant configuration, it can include cake transfer, face trimming, horizontal and vertical wire cutting, bottom-layer removal and cutting-waste recovery.

Is the AAC cake cut before or after autoclaving? +

In the process described here, the green cake is cut after pre-curing and demoulding but before autoclaving. Separate post-autoclave sawing equipment may be used for special finished-product applications.

What affects AAC block cutting accuracy? +

Important factors include cake condition, wire spacing and tension, blade setting, machine alignment, side-plate condition, cake movement and consistent upstream process control.

Can one cutting system produce different block sizes? +

A system can be configured for multiple sizes, but the supported range, wire arrangement and changeover method must be confirmed in the project specification.

How is cutting waste reused? +

Green cutting waste can be collected, converted into controlled waste slurry and returned to production. The usable proportion depends on the recipe, raw materials, slurry properties and quality-control results.

Can the system be installed in an existing AAC plant? +

Yes, subject to a technical assessment of cake size, side plates, layout, foundations, transfer arrangement, controls, waste recovery and the available shutdown period.
