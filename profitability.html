01 · UNDERSTAND PROFITABILITY

Installed capacity is not profit.
Saleable output is the starting point.

A project can look profitable at full rated output and still face pressure when demand ramps up slowly, prices fall, losses increase or customer payments are delayed.

01 Revenue

Saleable AAC volume × realised selling price per m³

02 Operating cost

Inputs, utilities, manpower, maintenance, losses, selling and freight support

03 Fixed & finance cost

Overheads, interest, depreciation, insurance and working-capital cost

THE COMMERCIAL REALITY

Installed capacity, actual production, saleable output, dispatched volume and collected revenue are not the same.

02 · EVALUATE THE PROJECT

Seven factors decide profitability.
One weak assumption can change the result.

Build the business case from current, location-specific inputs—not universal margins, payback claims or national growth headlines.

01

Regional demand

Measure realistic AAC consumption, competing supply and buyer acceptance inside the proposed sales territory.

02

Delivered price

Calculate the realised price after discounts, dealer margins, freight support, breakage claims and credit costs.

03

Raw materials

Verify suitability, consistency, landed cost and availability of fly ash or sand and every major process input.

04

Capacity utilisation

Model commissioning, ramp-up, downtime, maintenance, product losses and seasonal changes—not only full output.

05

Quality & reliability

Consistent saleable production matters more than rated capacity. Process control and maintenance shape unit economics.

06

Working capital

Plan inventory, wages, utilities, finished stock, customer credit, freight and delayed collections during ramp-up.

07

Capacity & automation

Match plant size and automation to demand, manpower, technical support and the practical expansion path.

DOWNSIDE TEST

Test the project against lower sales, a reduced realised price, higher input cost and a longer customer-credit period.

03 · AVOID THE EXTREMES

Two investment traps.
One balanced decision.

The lowest initial investment and highest installed capacity can both create problems. Plant size should follow realistic market ramp-up.

RISK 01

The Small Plant Trap

When output is limited, supervision, laboratory, boiler operation, maintenance, administration and sales costs are distributed across fewer saleable cubic metres.

Low investment does not automatically mean low unit cost.

RISK 02

The Over-Investment Trap

Capacity or automation beyond realistic market absorption can create high break-even volume, debt servicing and working-capital pressure during ramp-up.

Maximum capacity is not automatically the right capacity.

CAPACITY PRINCIPLE

Plan a commercially balanced and expansion-ready plant—then align utilities, layout and automation with that decision.

02

03

04

Protect expansion
