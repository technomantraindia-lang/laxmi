Mould handling.
Engineered as a cycle.

A balanced AAC mould handling and precuring system connects pouring, initial setting, tilting, cutting and side-plate return—without treating each machine as an isolated purchase.

DIRECT ANSWER

More than mould movement.

An AAC mould handling and precuring system moves filled moulds from pouring to controlled initial setting, then positions the green cake for demoulding and cutting.

Its engineering purpose is to keep moulds, side plates, transfer equipment and downstream machines circulating at the rate required by the production plan. If one part is undersized or poorly sequenced, it can restrict otherwise capable equipment.

THE CENTRAL ENGINEERING QUESTION

Is every mould ready when pouring needs it—and every side plate available when production needs it?

THE COMPLETE CYCLE

From empty mould
to cutting-ready cake.

The process is continuous. Each movement affects the next pour, the cutting line and the availability of production carriers downstream.

  1. 01

    Prepare

    Clean, inspect and apply release oil to the empty mould.

  2. 02

    Pour

    Position the mould and receive the prepared AAC mix.

  3. 03

    Transfer

    Move the filled mould into the designated precuring area.

  4. 04

    Pre-cure

    Allow the cake to rise and develop cutting-ready green strength.

  5. 05

    Tilt

    Position the mould and green cake for demoulding and cutting.

  6. 06

    Separate cycles

    Return the mould; continue the side plate with the cake.

PRECURING CONTROL

Time is a reference.
Readiness is the decision.

Precuring allows the poured material to rise and develop the green strength required for demoulding and wire cutting.

Industry references often describe approximately two to three hours. But actual cutting readiness depends on the raw-material source, recipe, slurry temperature, aluminium reaction, cake size and process conditions. It is not a universal fixed guarantee.

Controlled conditions, not an assumed setpoint

A closed or controlled enclosure may help where ambient variation affects production. The heating method, circulation, control point and operating range must be established during process engineering. Recoverable heat may be considered only through an engineered heat-exchange arrangement with the necessary steam-balance and safety review.

THE KEY DISTINCTION

Mould cycle ≠
side-plate cycle.

The two items perform different functions and remain occupied for different lengths of time. Their quantities should therefore be calculated separately.

Circulation item Main stages Primary quantity driver
Mould box Pouring → precuring → tilting → cleaning/oiling → return Mould-cycle time and pouring interval
Side plate Cutting → handling → autoclave loading → autoclaving → unloading → return Complete downstream and autoclave schedule

The precise ratio is project-specific. It depends on the cutting route, autoclave arrangement, loading pattern, production shifts and side-plate return path.

AUTOMATE INTELLIGENTLY

Choose the control level.
Define what remains manual.

“Automatic” is meaningful only when movement, positioning, confirmation, interlocking and recovery functions are clearly described.

01

Manual

Operator-led movement where cycle demand and the risk assessment permit manual involvement.

Check labour dependency, ergonomics, safe movement and future capacity.
02

Semi-automatic

Powered movement at selected points while some sequencing remains operator controlled.

Check remaining decisions, position confirmation and interface bottlenecks.
03

Fully automatic

Interlinked drives, sensors and logic sequence mould movement between defined positions.

Check controls skill, fault recovery, sensor reliability and maintenance discipline.

BUYER CHECK

A motorised machine is not automatically a fully integrated system.

Ask for position confirmation, permissives, collision prevention, alarms, manual and maintenance modes, fault recovery and plant-wide PLC integration.

COMMON DESIGN ERRORS

What appears small
can restrict the whole line.

  1. 01

    Selecting mould quantity only from daily capacity.

  2. 02

    Assuming mould and side-plate quantities are equal.

  3. 03

    Treating precuring as a fixed waiting time.

  4. 04

    Motorising movement without sequencing and interlocks.

  5. 05

    Ignoring expansion space in the original track layout.

  6. 06

    Accepting poor rail and transfer-interface alignment.

PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

Before you finalise
the handling section.

01What is the purpose of precuring in an AAC plant?

Precuring allows the poured AAC mix to rise and develop sufficient green strength for the selected demoulding and wire-cutting process.

02How long does AAC precuring take?

Industry references often describe approximately two to three hours, but actual cutting readiness depends on raw materials, recipe, temperature, cake size and the plant's approved control method. It should not be treated as a universal fixed time.

03What temperature should a precuring room maintain?

There is no universal setpoint for every AAC plant. The operating range and measurement point should be established from the raw-material system, mix behaviour, enclosure design and validated production trials.

04How is the required number of moulds calculated?

The preliminary quantity is based on total mould-cycle time divided by the planned pouring interval, then checked against physical positions, operating allowance, maintenance and standby requirements.

05Why are more side plates than moulds often required?

The mould can return after demoulding, while the side plate may remain occupied through cutting, autoclaving and return. The two circulation quantities must therefore be calculated separately.

06Can an existing mould-handling system be upgraded?

A retrofit may be possible after reviewing track condition, layout, mould interfaces, production sequence, controls, civil constraints and the required capacity improvement.
