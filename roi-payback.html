01 · Understand

Profit is not produced by capacity.
It is produced by saleable output.

Installed capacity is a technical rating. It is not automatically the volume produced, sold or converted into cash.

A credible analysis connects engineering performance with regional demand and financial discipline.

01

Rated capacity

Configured output under defined operating conditions.

02

Achievable production

Output after operating days, availability, ramp-up and process efficiency.

03

Saleable production

Dispatchable output after rejection, breakage and quality losses.

04

Collected revenue

Cash realised after deductions, credit period and collections.

02 · Evaluate

Four realities.
One investment case.

Test every factor using visible assumptions. A change in one input can alter plant scope, working capital and financial exposure.

01

Total capital employed

Machinery, civil works, utilities, installation, pre-operative expenses, contingency and working capital.

02

Saleable production

Rated capacity adjusted for operating days, availability, efficiency, rejection, breakage and sales utilisation.

03

Net sales realisation

Invoice value after discounts, freight support, dealer incentives, credit cost and selling deductions.

04

Operating cash generation

Revenue less raw materials, steam, power, manpower, maintenance, administration and distribution cost.

“How much AAC can the regional market absorb—at what net realisation, operating cost and working-capital cycle?”

The decision framework

Four metrics.
Four different questions.

ROI, break-even, payback and IRR are related, but not interchangeable. Each requires a clear formula and assumption base.

Metric Question answered Calculation principle
ROI What annual return is generated? Defined annual return ÷ investment base × 100
Break-even How much AAC must be sold? Fixed cost ÷ contribution per saleable m³
Payback When is invested cash recovered? When cumulative project cash flow becomes positive
IRR What discount rate makes NPV zero? Timing and amount of multi-year cash flows

03 · Decide

Stress-test the project.
Then select the plant.

Compare configurations using the same market, cost, financing and ramp-up assumptions.

Downside

Can the project withstand delay?

Slower sales, lower realisation, higher input cost and longer collections.

Base case

Is the plan evidence-led?

Defensible demand, practical utilisation and realistic working capital.

Upside

What creates additional value?

Faster absorption, stronger product mix and better efficiency.

Costs commonly missed

Small omissions.
Large cash-flow pressure.

  2. Customer credit and inventory
  3. Freight, incentives and unloading
  4. Shutdown maintenance and major spares
  5. Market-development and compliance cost
