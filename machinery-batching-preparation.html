AAC Batching System & Raw Material Preparation

A controlled AAC batch begins before the mixer. Material preparation, slurry condition, calibrated measurement and the approved recipe must work as one connected process.

For fly-ash, pond-ash and sand-based AAC plants

DIRECT ANSWER

What is an AAC batching system?

An AAC batching system measures, sequences and mixes the slurry, cement, lime, gypsum, water, aluminium and other approved ingredients required for each production batch.

It connects raw-material preparation with mould filling through storage, weighing, dosing, pumps, valves, conveyors, the batching mixer and the selected level of PLC-based recipe control.

A mixer cannot correct an unsuitable raw material or an incorrectly measured batch.

Reliable production starts with testing, controlled preparation and calibrated measurement.

THREE MATERIAL ROUTES

The raw material changes the preparation system.

A standard machine list should not be applied before the available siliceous material is evaluated.

01

Suitable dry fly ash

Controlled transfer → Slurry preparation → Screening → Density verification → Storage

Review fineness, chemistry, transfer method, foreign matter and slurry consistency before freezing the system.

02

Pond ash or bottom ash

Bulk feeding → Preparation or grinding → Screening → Density verification → Storage

Suitability depends on representative testing, moisture, chemistry, particle size and production trials.

03

Sand

Feeding → Wet grinding → Screening → Density verification → Agitated storage

Required fineness, mill duty, slurry temperature, storage residence and sedimentation must be evaluated together.

THE CONTROL INSIGHT

Equal slurry volume does not always mean equal solids.

When slurry concentration changes, the amount of fly ash or sand entering a nominally identical batch can also change.

Density is therefore an important control indicator. However, density alone does not provide a universal solid-content value. The plant needs a material-specific and technically validated method for measurement, sampling and recipe correction.

Density / concentration → Material quantity → Batch consistency

SYSTEM SCOPE

One process. Five connected functions.

The final scope depends on raw material, batch volume, plant layout and the required automation philosophy.

01

Storage

Cement, lime and gypsum silos; slurry tanks; level instruments and dust-control provisions.

02

Preparation

Fly-ash or ash feeding, sand grinding where required, slurry preparation, screening and agitation.

03

Measurement

Weigh hoppers, load cells, controlled feeders, valves, pumps and verified slurry measurement.

04

Mixing

AAC batching mixer, controlled material sequence, aluminium addition and mould-filling interface.

05

Automation

PLC/HMI, recipe permissions, tolerances, alarms, interlocks and batch records as specified.

BATCH SEQUENCE

Controlled from recipe selection to mould filling.

The exact order and timing must be validated for the approved recipe and plant configuration.

  1. 01

    Confirm batch conditions

    Verify the selected recipe, material availability and essential equipment status.

  2. 02

    Measure the ingredients

    Weigh or dose slurry and dry materials through the configured measuring devices.

  3. 03

    Follow the material sequence

    Transfer slurry, cement, lime, gypsum, water and approved additives in the defined order.

  4. 04

    Mix under validated conditions

    Complete the initial mixing stage while monitoring the required process conditions.

  5. 05

    Add aluminium at the validated stage

    The quantity, preparation method, addition point and final mixing duration are project-specific.

  6. 06

    Discharge and record

    Permit mould filling when required interlocks are satisfied and retain the necessary batch data.

AUTOMATION WITHOUT MYTHS

A PLC does not automatically make a plant “fully automatic.”

The word automatic should describe the functions measured, sequenced, interlocked and recorded—not merely the presence of a control panel.

Function What the buyer should verify
Recipe control Approved recipes, access permissions and change history
Automatic weighing Target and actual values, tolerances, refill logic and calibration method
Sequence control Order of addition, mixing stages, timeout handling and discharge permissives
Aluminium dosing Preparation method, quantity control, addition permissive and safe handling
Batch reporting Batch number, recipe, target/actual quantities, timings, alarms and operator actions

DECISION RISKS

Common mistakes that create avoidable process problems.

Common mistake Why it matters Better approach
Selecting machinery before testing materials The preparation route and recipe may not suit the available raw material. Test representative materials before freezing the scope.
Treating slurry volume as constant solid quantity A concentration change can alter the actual solids delivered. Use a validated density/concentration relationship and measurement method.
Using one aluminium timing for every recipe Reaction conditions vary with material, mixer and process. Validate quantity, addition stage and final mixing during commissioning.
Calling any PLC system fully automatic Important decisions may still depend on manual intervention. Define weighing, sequencing, interlocks, records and exception handling.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION

Finished-product consistency depends on raw-material characteristics, recipe development, calibration, operating conditions and plant maintenance. Machinery alone cannot guarantee a specific block result.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions serious AAC investors should ask.

Clear definitions reduce the risk of comparing systems by labels alone.

What is an AAC batching system?+

It is the group of equipment and controls used to measure, sequence and mix slurry, cement, lime, gypsum, water, aluminium and other approved ingredients for each AAC batch. The exact scope depends on the raw material, capacity, layout and automation requirement.

Why is slurry density important?+

A change in slurry concentration can change the amount of solid material delivered in a batch. Density is therefore an important control indicator, but its relationship with solid content must be validated for the specific material.

Can pond ash or bottom ash be used?+

It may be considered only when representative testing and production trials establish suitability. Moisture, chemistry, particle-size distribution, carbon or organic matter and consistency should be assessed.

When is aluminium added to the mixer?+

Aluminium is normally introduced toward the final part of the validated mixing sequence. Its quantity, preparation method, addition point and final mixing duration are specific to the material, recipe, mixer and operating conditions.

What makes a batching system automatic?+

A properly defined automatic system may include recipe selection, automatic weighing, sequence control, tolerance checks, alarms, interlocks, controlled aluminium dosing and batch reporting. The included functions should be verified individually.

Can an existing batching system be upgraded?+

An upgrade may be possible after reviewing the mechanical equipment, weighing arrangement, controls, available space, cycle requirement and integration with the rest of the plant.
