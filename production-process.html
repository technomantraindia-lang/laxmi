AAC Block & Panel Production Process

Every AAC product begins
with a controlled process.

AAC blocks and reinforced panels are produced through a connected sequence of raw-material preparation, batching, casting, rising, cutting and high-pressure steam curing. The correct plant configuration begins with the actual raw materials and intended product—not a standard machinery list.

How AAC is manufactured

One connected process.
Not twelve separate machines.

The process converts a prepared mineral mix into a cellular green cake, cuts it to size and develops final properties through autoclave curing.

Each stage creates the operating condition required by the next. A raw-material variation may appear later as unstable rising, cutting damage or inconsistent cured product.

That is why process engineering connects material testing, equipment selection, instrumentation, operating logic and quality checks as one system.

Material qualification→ Preparation→ Formation→ Cutting→ Autoclaving→ Accepted product
01

Receive & qualify

Confirm the selected silica source, lime, cement, gypsum and aluminium system against representative material tests before defining the process route.

Why it matters: Prevents equipment and recipe decisions from being based on assumed material behaviour.
02

Prepare the silica source

Store dry fly ash, prepare pond-ash slurry or grind silica sand according to the route justified by the tested material.

Why it matters: Determines major equipment, power, water, storage and process-control requirements.
03

Prepare & stabilise slurry

Control solids, density, agitation and recirculation so the batching system receives a repeatable feed rather than a changing mixture.

Why it matters: Improves recipe repeatability and reduces avoidable variation between moulds.
04

Weigh & batch

Measure each constituent to the approved recipe and maintain a defined sequence of addition, mixing time and casting quantity.

Why it matters: Batching accuracy influences reaction behaviour, density, strength and material consumption.
05

Mix & cast

Create a uniform mix, introduce the aerating agent under controlled conditions and transfer the batch into the mould within the required process window.

Why it matters: Timing and temperature affect pore formation, rise behaviour and the stability of the cast mass.
06

Reinforce panels where required

Fabricate, protect, position and secure the engineered reinforcement system before or during casting, according to the selected panel process.

Why it matters: Panel performance depends on product design and reinforcement control—not simply adding steel to a block line.
07

Rise & pre-cure

Allow the cast mass to expand and develop sufficient green strength under controlled ambient and process conditions.

Why it matters: Cutting too early can deform the cake; cutting too late can increase cutting resistance and damage risk.
08

Demould & cut

Demould, position and cut the green cake to the required block or panel dimensions while recovering suitable process waste where the configuration permits.

Why it matters: Cutting condition influences dimensional accuracy, surface quality and downstream handling losses.
09

Load the autoclave

Arrange the cut products and loading system so steam circulation, load stability and safe movement are addressed.

Why it matters: Loading practice affects cycle consistency, product protection and autoclave utilisation.
10

Autoclave

Apply the validated high-pressure saturated-steam cycle, including controlled pressure rise, holding and release.

Why it matters: The cycle must suit the raw materials, product, loading and equipment design; one universal setting does not apply.
11

Separate & inspect

Separate cured products where required, verify defined quality checks and isolate non-conforming material before packing.

Why it matters: Saleable output matters more than nominal mould volume when evaluating plant performance.
12

Pack & dispatch

Identify, protect, handle and pack accepted blocks or panels for the intended storage and transport method.

Why it matters: Finished-product damage can erase value created by an otherwise controlled production process.

Engineering principle

Trace the affected batch. Verify the measurement. Isolate the contributors. Change one controlled variable. Validate the outcome.

Decide with evidence

What should an investor verify
before selecting machinery?

  1. Test the actual raw materials

    Use representative samples and define the required preparation route before finalising equipment.

  2. Define the product mix

    Decide whether the plant must produce blocks, reinforced panels or both—and what that adds to the line.

  3. Evaluate saleable capacity

    Check the complete cycle, bottlenecks and quality losses rather than relying on mould volume alone.

  4. Map the complete machinery scope

    Connect each production stage with its equipment, auxiliary systems, controls and responsibility boundary.

  5. Define the automation boundary

    Ask what is manual, mechanised, automatically controlled, interlinked, instrumented and recorded.

  6. Protect future expansion

    Reserve justified space and utility capacity for additional moulds, autoclaves, panel systems or packing automation.

Investor questions

Direct answers.
Without oversimplification.

Can one AAC plant manufacture both blocks and panels?+

A plant may be engineered for both, but a block-only line is not automatically panel-ready. Reinforcement preparation, cage handling, cutting configuration, product handling, engineering design and testing must be evaluated.

Does dry fly ash require grinding?+

Not necessarily. It may bypass grinding when representative testing confirms that its fineness, chemistry and consistency suit the validated AAC process.

Why does pond ash require preparation?+

Pond ash may contain coarse particles, variable moisture and unwanted material. Screening, slurry preparation and controlled storage may be required to provide a consistent feed for batching.

Does autoclaving create the air pores?+

No. The cellular pore structure develops during rising. Autoclaving provides hydrothermal curing that develops the final material structure and product properties.

What pressure and temperature are required for AAC autoclaving?+

There is no single universal setting. The approved cycle depends on raw materials, product dimensions, loading, recipe and equipment design, and must be established during commissioning.

What should an investor provide before a technical discussion?+

The most useful inputs are the project location, raw-material sources and test reports, intended products, target capacity, land, utilities, preferred automation level and expansion plan.
